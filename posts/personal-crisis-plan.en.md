I recently read 426’s views on the future of Japan and the possibility of a global economic crisis. I also came across many predictions about major disasters striking Japan this year. It made me think that the “big one” people have long talked about may indeed be approaching. I have never experienced an economic crisis, nor am I particularly familiar with earthquakes and other disasters. All I can do now is prepare for everything I can think of, which may at least give me some peace of mind.<br>　　First, I need to consider immediate personal safety, including earthquakes, tsunamis, and fires. I do not know enough about these disasters. Even if one occurred nearby, I could not guarantee that I would react in time or immediately take the correct action. Therefore, while searching for a place to live, I have been using location and price to identify relatively safe areas, hoping to compensate as much as possible for my lack of disaster-preparedness knowledge. Of course, I still need to learn about disaster preparedness, but I may not need to meet the same stringent standards as someone living in a high-risk area. I can also take additional precautions to protect the things in my room and reduce potential property losses.<br>　　Once I have made sure that I can survive a disaster in the natural world, the next thing I need to face is a disaster in the financial world. Having never experienced an economic crisis, I cannot truly imagine how severe the downturn might become, how difficult life might get, or what I could do beyond ensuring that I still have enough resources to sustain myself through the coldest part of the economic winter.<br>　　There are two aspects to this: maintaining my Japanese work visa so that I will not be forced to leave the country, and increasing my income so that I can support myself while also putting myself in a stronger position to benefit from the boom that may follow the economic crisis.<br>　　First is the work visa. The main reason for maintaining it is that I am already in Japan, and keeping my current employment is the lowest-cost option. Other countries are also reducing immigration, and given my personal interests, remaining in Japan would likewise involve the least disruption. While maintaining my work visa, it would be best to obtain permanent residency as well. This would reduce many of the costs and constraints of living in Japan and give me greater freedom in choosing how to earn an income. The fastest option would, of course, be the one-year route for Highly Skilled Professionals with 80 points, although there would still be a considerable wait after applying. Assuming I change jobs by the end of this year and can reach 80 points with the other qualifying items 【15+10+15+5\*2+15+15】, if I then need to wait another one or two years after applying, I may not receive permanent residency until the end of 2028 at the latest.<br>　　Next is income. In the future, I hope to have income from two sources. One would be salaried employment. The nature of the work does not matter, although I would prefer something that allows me to work from home and have more personal time. The other would be side income or passive income.<br>　　When it comes to making money, I believe that, excluding dishonest or improper methods, the only way is to satisfy other people’s needs: give and take. People choose to spend money to fulfill their needs. In the case of employment, a company turns its needs into a specific position, and I provide my labor in exchange for a stable cash flow. However, a job consumes a significant amount of time and energy, indirectly reducing the time available to explore other possibilities. This is the advantage of working from home: it makes it possible to redistribute my personal time. Another way to earn money is to create a product that other people can pay for because it meets their needs. Both approaches involve satisfying other people’s needs. With a job, I must continuously provide my time over the long term, but I receive a stable cash flow in return. When selling a product, I only need to make a major creative investment once, followed by a relatively small amount of ongoing maintenance, potentially producing long-term, continuous cash flow. The prerequisite, however, is thorough market research and promotion to ensure that the product is competitive within the area of demand it targets. In other words, one approach involves selling my time and labor, while the other involves selling my assets.<br>　　After accumulating a certain amount of personal assets, the next question will be how to manage and invest them so that money can generate more money. Of course, it may be better to think about this systematically only after my assets exceed the 100k level. With fewer assets, I might be more inclined toward a high-risk portfolio and therefore more likely to lose my principal. Still, investing a small amount of spare money in the stock market to develop my trading skills could also be a worthwhile direction.
