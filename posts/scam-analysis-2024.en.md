## The Scam

Notice: Payment for sending the script has increased from RMB 3 to RMB 8.<br>           Payment for a completed form has increased from RMB 90 to RMB 160.

👍👍New rates: Sending the script counts as one job and pays RMB 8; each customer who completes the form pays RMB 160.

Instructions:

	1. WhatsApp is a mainstream foreign social app, similar to WeChat. We need to attract users manually: I give you designated numbers, and you send them our recruitment ads as a follow-up. You do not need to add contacts on WhatsApp; like Telegram, you can message someone directly if you have their number. Sending the script pays RMB 3 per job, and if you send the customer a form and they complete it, you receive RMB 90.
	2. Buy an old US WhatsApp account. It must be an aged account; newly registered accounts get banned instantly, so do not waste your money. An account can normally be used for more than ten days without being banned, so you do not need to worry about losing it. If you do not know an account seller, customer support can recommend one.<br>(We mainly target users in the US, so the WhatsApp account must be American. Do not use an account from another country.)
	3. Set the name and profile picture as instructed by customer support. You must change both, or the job will not count.
	4. Follow our script and use the specified data exactly. Anyone thinking of submitting fake jobs can give it a try—we will deduct everything.
	5. The data is collected from local recruitment-ad websites. These are all people looking for part-time work. We are only conducting a simple follow-up, and approximately 3 out of every 10 jobs succeed.
	6. Messaging a contact with the script pays RMB 3 per job. Then send them a form; if they complete it, you receive RMB 90.
	7. To submit a job, provide one screenshot of the chat interface with the customer. That completes one RMB 3 job. If you send the customer a form and they complete it, you receive RMB 90; if they do not, you receive only RMB 3. Even one account can generate a minimum daily profit of RMB 800+.
	8. Working hours are 24/7, with better results at night.

## Scam Summary

The entire scam was riddled with red flags. I had identified some of them while researching the project beforehand, but I was still too naive. I thought the project’s only exploitative aspects were the website’s cut and the division of task earnings. I never imagined that the project was merely bait and that the real goal was to lure me onto that [website](https://gacc327.com/). Perhaps the project’s manager also runs the website, or perhaps they are merely a referrer, but that no longer matters. What matters is that they led me to the website, and my desire for money led me to transfer money to them. That completes their revenue loop: they entice people who are short of money, desperate for money, or hoping to get something for nothing into paying them, thereby transferring money directly into their accounts. They even provide 24/7 customer support specifically to funnel people toward the most expensive offering. Seriously, it almost brings me to tears.

(Damn it, the more I think about it, the angrier I get. They are even more ruthless than a honeypot: not only do they swallow your money, but they also make you work for them and lure even more people into the scam. In that sense, they are even more malicious than rug pullers. Apart from outright honeypots, rug pullers at least put in some money as a gesture and wait until the price cannot climb any higher before running off. Viewed this way, the blockchain really is the most transparent environment: whenever someone does something, it is permanently recorded on-chain, and you can identify who scammed you. Off-chain, even after being scammed, information asymmetry may leave you helping the scammers count their money.

The scam’s entire business model is therefore to have people lure those who want money onto their website. Once someone pays on the website, that money can no longer be withdrawn. Most people do not notice because they make a purchase immediately after depositing funds. The scammers then arrange for people to discover that they cannot log in after buying an account, allowing them to make their victims spend money step by step in the sequence of account → dedicated-line IP → data-center IP. Since I did not go through their entire scam funnel, I do not know what other stages they use to entice people. I can make an assumption, though: perhaps withdrawals of referral earnings still work, allowing victims of the website to become downstream affiliates who recruit new users for them. (Come to think of it, Telegram accounts are the cheapest item on their website, so even people who want to become their affiliates must first continue sending them money? That is seriously fucking unscrupulous.) Of course, even after someone earns money by recruiting new users, the site might not let them withdraw it directly. It could first impose a high withdrawal threshold, then apply a worse rmb2usd exchange rate and exorbitant fees, causing withdrawals of smaller amounts to lose a full 20%-50% of their value.

(Thinking about it this way, this environment is even worse than crypto. Even in crypto PvP, there is at least a tacit understanding that people will not dump immediately at the outset—dumping on paper hands is another matter. Here, after giving them your money, they manipulate you into working for them because of your sunk costs.

Of course, what they ultimately provide might genuinely work, but in reality this setup does not cost anywhere near that much. There were also problems with customer support’s answers. They used terminology such as “dedicated-line IP” to attract attention and make people regard their so-called dedicated-line IP as categorically different from the proxy or VPN services people normally use. In practice, however, there is no functional difference between the two. The only differences lie in the IP address itself and its network route. So when I showed customer support my Shadowrocket configuration, how could they tell at a glance that I was not using a dedicated-line IP? They performed no tests and flatly asserted it as soon as they saw the image I sent. That was their first giveaway. The second was how they communicated with me after I switched to a new account. Even if the same support agent handled both conversations, they should not have immediately treated an entirely different account as the previous customer. Incidentally, if they mistook the person speaking to them for me, does that mean I was the only person they had scammed recently? Honestly, that is a little sad. The customer-support and technical-support accounts may also have been operated by the same person, judging by various details. When I pretended to be a different user, they clearly did not realize what was happening. They may have logged out of the technical account, switched to the support account to check, and then switched back. The technical account went offline for a minute or two, appeared online again, and then went offline once more. Together, these two giveaways made my distrust of them peak and helped me identify all the earlier things that had felt wrong. They may genuinely have products, but now that I no longer trust them, I will not buy anything from them again. Some of their products are also severely overpriced, and I have no need to be a lamb led to slaughter.

Ultimately, the main reason I fell for such a crude scam was that their bait stirred up the desperation created by my lack of money. Even though I researched the project beforehand, I still chose to enter it thinking, “I’ll just regard whatever money I put in as lost to a rug and see how it goes.” After entering, the sunk-cost effect took over, and I invested far too much. Thinking about it this way, as long as bait that makes me want easy money exists, it will influence me and make me continue investing. Therefore, I cannot trust myself.

The lesson from this experience is that, as a human being, I will inevitably be influenced by all kinds of desires. If someone guides me by appealing to a particular desire, I will certainly be affected. At such times, I need to restrain myself through rational thought. I must first consider the investment’s rate of return, the safety of my principal after investing, and whether the project’s investment pathway can genuinely produce returns. In addition, even if the project is genuinely viable, I should consider whether other projects offer higher returns, require less work, and provide better overall value. In short, I hope I lose less money the next time I get scammed. Come to think of it, the fact that I can remain so calm after being scammed and losing money may indeed be something the crypto world gave me: the belief that even if I take a loss now, I can still earn it back later.
