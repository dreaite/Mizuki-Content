I realized that I have actually been suppressing myself.

I became aware of this when external stimuli stirred excitement and impulses within me. As soon as I noticed their influence, I reflexively suppressed the impulses they had created, keeping my emotions in a steady state.

I think this may have been influenced by an idea I formed long ago: that I was still inside a cocoon. When I was at school, I believed that the environment there was harmful to me, so I chose to isolate my deeper self from interacting with it. Now that I have come here, I feel that my surroundings are somewhat better, and I also have more freedom to choose what those surroundings are like. So perhaps I should open myself up more and allow myself to truly grow.

But my previous way of life seems to have become a habit. I am afraid of engaging with the outside world, and afraid of letting my inner self break away from this artificially maintained equilibrium. And so I have begun, unconsciously and of my own accord, to preserve this stillness—so much so that the past year of my life could be described as a sludge-like, deathly stagnation.

Today, here, I realized that I am actually still inside the cocoon.

So what should I do? I have become somewhat aware of my current situation, yet I am still unable to escape it. I have always believed that only after figuring out who I truly am would I have the confidence to face the outside world. But how can I discover my true self without continually coming into contact with all kinds of things, while remaining trapped in this pool of deathly stillness? This is probably my predicament.

It is true, however, that the self which exists in the world is not there from the beginning. Rather, its possible form is determined through its interactions with the world.

But the self I am searching for is actually something more fundamental: the most primal impulses that arise from me simply as myself. That is what I want to find. Of course, I do not mean that impulse can point in only one direction, nor that there can be only one kind of impulse that gives me the desire to move forward. But I feel that, since human beings are bound toward a future, the decisions I make that determine where these future impulses will lead also determine my future.<br>So perhaps I am also being weighed down by this very possibility.

Choosing a direction inherently means having to relinquish certain other possibilities. Of course, I also know that these paths will not disappear simply because I choose another one, and that I may still explore them again in the future. But the choice I make at this moment brings about a genuine change in me: it changes my brain and filters the direction of my future experiences. Perhaps that is why I am afraid of making this choice and losing other possible versions of myself.

Thinking about it this way, what I fear most is actually the responsibility of choosing a direction and moving forward. I am afraid of being responsible for that choice.

In that case, the answer is actually right in front of me.
